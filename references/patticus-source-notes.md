# Market Command Matrix — Patticus Source Notes

Source: Patrick Campbell / Patticus, “Competitor Research and Strategy” (2023): https://patticus.com/2023/12/16/competitive-research-playbook/

## Key additions to preserve

- Competitor research is for judgement before action, not instant tactical output: “intelligence is upstream of action.”
- The customer’s view of the market matters more than internal direct/indirect/substitute labels.
- Market mindshare should ideally come from unaided customer recall, not internal anxiety or boardroom repetition.
- Resource strength means resources dedicated to winning the specific market, not total company size.
- A large broad player can be resource-weak in a niche; a focused small player can be resource-strong.

## Reconnaissance method

Core unaided question:

> When you think of [CATEGORY], what is the first product that comes to mind?

Aided follow-ups:

- Which [CATEGORY] companies are you familiar with?
- What do you currently use?
- How likely are you to recommend the company/product you use?
- What is most valuable and least valuable about it?

Respondent sources, easiest to hardest:

1. Customers and prospects in the database, ideally anonymous to reduce bias.
2. Paid market panelists.
3. Prospects outside the database, subject to compliance.
4. Competitor customers found via public logos, case studies, reviews, and social proof.
5. Targeted ads to survey respondents.

Bias controls:

- Ask unaided before aided.
- Avoid anchoring around the brand or a named competitor.
- Use neutral or anonymous research framing where possible.
- Avoid raffles unless respondents are directly paid.

## Market shapes

- **Intensely fragmented:** no clear winner. Grow fast if the market is valuable; reconsider if small/low value.
- **Challenger:** one or two big players dominate. Attack the leader; consider partner attacks with other challengers.
- **Ancient:** old/stagnant incumbents and poor customer experience. Harvest aggressively.
- **Mature:** active entrants/exits and competitors across quadrants. Run offensive and defensive playbooks.
